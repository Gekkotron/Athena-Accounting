# Two-factor authentication (TOTP)

Add a second factor to your account login so a leaked password alone can
never take over your Athena. TOTP is **optional and per-user** — each
account decides for itself whether to enrol, and the extra step only
appears at sign-in for the accounts that opted in.

Athena's 2FA uses the RFC 6238 TOTP standard: a 6-digit code that
rotates every 30 seconds, generated by any authenticator app you
already use (Google Authenticator, Aegis, 1Password, Bitwarden,
Ente Auth, …). No SMS, no email, no push — just the app on your
phone (or a hardware key that emulates one).

## When to enrol

The single-user home-server setup on a trusted LAN is already tolerable
without 2FA: physical access controls the perimeter and Athena is not
exposed to the public internet. Enrol if any of the following apply:

- You reused the Athena password anywhere else (a breach elsewhere then
  hands the attacker a working login here).
- Someone on the LAN could get a look at a written or saved password.
- You exposed Athena beyond the LAN (a reverse tunnel, a Tailscale/VPN
  bridge, an nginx port-forward on your router).
- You just want the extra assurance — enrolment takes about a minute
  and disabling it later is a few clicks.

## Enrolling

*Réglages → Sécurité → Double authentification (2FA) → Activer*.

1. **Confirm your password.** Athena asks for your current account
   password before generating the secret. This defends against a
   stolen session silently enrolling a factor an attacker controls
   and locking you out of your own account.
2. **Scan the QR code.** Open your authenticator app, add a new account,
   and scan the QR. If the app can't read a QR (a hardware
   authenticator, a headless setup), the **manual key** below the QR
   is the same secret in base32 — type it into the app instead.
3. **Enter the current 6-digit code** shown by the app and click
   **Vérifier**. This proves the secret made it into the app before
   Athena flips the account into "2FA active" state.
4. **Save your recovery codes.** Ten single-use codes are shown once,
   right after the verify step. Print them, paste them into your
   password manager, save them to an encrypted file — anywhere that
   survives losing your phone. You cannot see them again after closing
   the modal, but you can regenerate a fresh set at any time (which
   invalidates the previous ones).

Closing the modal before the final step abandons the enrolment: the
pending secret is discarded and 2FA stays off. Nothing to clean up.

## Signing in with 2FA

Once enrolled, the login flow gains a second step:

1. Type your username and password on the login screen, as usual.
2. When the password is correct, the screen swaps to a **6-digit code**
   field. Open your authenticator app and type the current code for
   Athena.
3. If you don't have the app to hand (lost phone, out of battery),
   click **Utiliser un code de récupération** and type one of the
   codes you saved at enrolment. Codes are single-use — the count in
   Settings drops by one each time you burn one.
4. The **Se déconnecter** link at the bottom returns you to step 1
   without completing the second factor — useful if you started to
   log in on the wrong account.

The 6-digit code has ±30 s of slop, so a small clock drift between
your phone and the Athena server does not fail the check.

## Recovery codes

- **10 codes, single-use each.** Once a code is used to sign in, it
  cannot be used again. The count of remaining codes is shown in
  *Réglages → Sécurité → Double authentification*.
- **They are hashed at rest** (argon2id, same parameters as your
  password). Even someone with database access cannot recover the
  original codes.
- **When you run out**, TOTP still works — only the recovery path is
  gone. Regenerate a fresh set from the Security page.
- **Regenerating invalidates every previous code immediately** and
  requires re-entering your account password. You'll see the same "save
  them once" modal you saw at enrolment.

Losing your phone **and** your recovery codes at the same time locks
you out of the account permanently — the secret is encrypted at rest
and cannot be reset without server-side access. Keep the codes
somewhere separate from the device that generates the TOTP codes.

## Disabling

*Réglages → Sécurité → Double authentification → Désactiver*.

Disabling requires **both** your current account password **and** a
valid TOTP or recovery code. Neither alone is enough — this way a
leaked password can't turn 2FA off on its own, and a stolen unlocked
device can't disable it either.

On success, both the encrypted secret and every recovery code are
deleted from the database. Re-enrolling later goes through the same
flow from scratch and generates a fresh secret + fresh codes.

## Backups and moving Athena to a new machine

**TOTP is deliberately not included in backups.** The account is
tied to the specific Athena installation that generated the secret,
in the same way a browser session cookie is tied to a specific
browser. Restoring a JSON dump into a fresh Athena install carries
over your transactions, accounts, categories, rules, budgets, and
attachments — but the 2FA state is left off.

The consequence: **after restoring to a new install, log in with your
password only and re-enrol 2FA from scratch**. Delete the old codes
from your authenticator app once the new secret is confirmed.

This is intentional — treating 2FA as portable would mean that
anyone who got a copy of a backup could clone the secret and
generate valid codes for your account. Keeping it per-install
requires the physical Athena machine to be part of the compromise.

## Where the secret lives

Your TOTP secret is stored **encrypted at rest** in the `user_totp`
table: AES-256-GCM with a key derived from your `SESSION_SECRET`
(HKDF-SHA256), and the user id is bound as additional authenticated
data. The plaintext secret only ever exists in memory long enough to
verify a submitted code.

Rotating `SESSION_SECRET` therefore invalidates every enrolled TOTP —
after a rotation, users see "Code invalide" until they re-enrol. This
is the same behaviour that already applies to session cookies and
bank-sync credentials; see the SECURITY.md notes for the rotation
runbook.

## What 2FA does — and does not — protect against

**Protects against:**

- **A leaked or reused password.** Someone with only the password
  cannot pass step 2.
- **Silent 2FA hijack from a stolen session cookie.** Enrolling,
  disabling, and regenerating codes all require the account password
  — a cookie alone cannot pivot into persistent access.

**Does not protect against:**

- **A compromise of the server itself.** Anyone with shell on the
  machine can read every user's `SESSION_SECRET` and decrypt every
  TOTP secret. Same story as bank-sync credentials — inherent to any
  self-hosted-with-server-side-secrets design.
- **Real-time phishing or MitM inside the LAN.** A live proxy could
  relay both the password and the current 6-digit code within its
  30-second window. HTTPS terminated by nginx is the mitigation for
  the MitM path.
- **A stolen phone with the authenticator unlocked.** Same class as
  "stolen password + stolen phone" — orthogonal to TOTP. Use a
  passcode on your phone and your authenticator app.
